Teams-o-lantern build

A foam pumpkin that rings like an incoming Teams call when someone walks up, while a purple EL-wire outline of the logo lights up with it. Work through the five phases in order. Each step adds one thing, so when something doesn't work you know exactly where to look.

Current design

  1. The EL wire is the only light. It traces the outer outline of the logo: both heads and the body. No T cut, no LED strip, no light box.
  2. One printed template (print_template.html) gives you the outline to groove, the three EL wire holes and the motion-sensor hole, at full size.

Safety

  • Unplug the USB cable before you touch any screw terminal.
  • Hot-knife the foam outdoors, wearing the respirator. Take a break every 30 minutes.
  • The EL inverter puts out about 100 V AC at very low current. Seal the cut end of the wire and don't handle it while it's lit.

Tools

Bench: 2 mm flat screwdriver, scissors, wire strippers or a sharp knife, hot glue gun, ruler, pencil, tape. Garage: the foam cutter kit, respirator, a wooden skewer.

A

Bench electronics

Wire and test everything on the table before the pumpkin gets involved. If a part is dead, you find out now.

Flash CircuitPython onto the board

  1. Download the UF2 for this exact board from circuitpython.org/board/adafruit_feather_rp2040_prop_maker. Use the latest stable release.
  2. Plug the board into the Mac with a USB-C cable.
  3. Hold BOOT, tap RESET, then let go of BOOT. A drive named RPI-RP2 appears.
  4. Drag the .uf2 file onto RPI-RP2. The board restarts and comes back as a drive named CIRCUITPY.

This is the RP2040 method. Double-clicking RESET, which older Feathers use, does nothing here.

Get the three libraries

  1. At circuitpython.org/libraries, download the bundle that matches your CircuitPython version. For 10.x firmware that's the "Bundle for Version 10.x".
  2. Unzip it. From its lib folder, copy neopixel.mpy, adafruit_pixelbuf.mpy and adafruit_ticks.mpy into the project's firmware/lib folder.

The bundle version has to match the firmware. A 9.x library on 10.x firmware fails at boot.

Install the firmware

From the repo folder, run this in Terminal (macOS). It checks the libraries are in place and copies everything in the right order, with code.py last.

zsh firmware/install_to_board.sh
You should seeThe status light on the board blinks amber for 60 seconds while the motion sensor warms up, then turns dim green.

To watch what it's doing, open a serial window. Exit with Ctrl-A, then K, then Y.

screen /dev/tty.usbmodem* 115200

Until the motion sensor is connected, its input floats and may fire a phantom trigger (the status light turns white). Ignore it for now.

Set up the motion sensor

  1. Pop the white dome off and read the three pin labels, then put the dome back. With the pins at the top and the dials at the bottom they read GND, OUT, VCC from left to right.
  2. Move the jumper to H (repeat trigger). It keeps the output on while someone is still moving, which is what makes the pumpkin ring again if they linger.
  3. Turn the delay dial (right) fully counter-clockwise: the shortest hold, about 3 seconds.
  4. Set the range dial (left) to the middle, about 5 m. On most boards clockwise shortens the range and counter-clockwise lengthens it, up to about 7 m. Some clones run the other way, so you'll confirm it with a walk test on the porch.

Connect the motion sensor

USB unplugged. Three jumper wires:

  • PIR VCC → servo header V+, the middle pin (female–female)
  • PIR GND → servo header G, the top pin (female–female)
  • PIR OUT → the Btn screw, 4th from the top (male–female, male end clamped under the screw)

The terminal labels are printed on the back of the board. From the top, the end nearest the SDA pin, the six screws are Neo, G, 5V, Btn, −, +. The servo header reads G, V+, Sig from the top. Sig stays free until A8.

TestPlug in and wait out the 60-second warm-up. Wave a hand at the sensor: the status light turns white and the serial window prints TRIGGER. Give it 10 seconds between waves; that's the cooldown.

Connect the speaker

The speaker has no wires, just two solder tabs with small eyelets. You'll add leads without soldering.

  1. Cut the pin off one end of two male–male jumpers. Strip about 1.5 cm from the cut ends.
  2. Push each bare end through a tab's eyelet, fold it back on itself and twist it tight around the tab. Tug it: it shouldn't move.
  3. USB unplugged. Clamp the pin ends under the bottom two screws, − and +. With one speaker, polarity doesn't matter.
  4. Once the test below passes, cover each tab joint in a blob of hot glue. It locks the twist and keeps the tabs from touching each other or the metal frame.
TestWave. You hear one ring of the Teams call sound.

Test the EL wire on its own

  1. Plug the EL wire's connector into the ABALDI inverter.
  2. Plug the inverter's USB plug into any USB charger. The wire glows purple and the inverter hums faintly. Both are normal.
  3. Unplug it. The next step cuts the inverter's USB cable, so this is your one chance to test these two parts by themselves.
If the connectors don't fit each otherCut the lead off the MaxLax battery pack 5 cm from its connector, and cut the inverter's output lead the same way. Twist red to red and black to black, then cover each joint completely in hot glue. The battery pack's connector is now your adapter. The inverter output is about 100 V, so leave no bare copper showing.

Connect the MOSFET module and the EL wire

The module is a switch: when the board's Sig pin goes high, it connects the inverter to power and the EL wire lights. Hold it as in the picture, with the J1 holes on the left and the two blue screw blocks on the right. The top block is the output (OUT− left, OUT+ right). The bottom block is the power input (DC− left, DC+ right).

Your modules come as a panel of six. Snap one off: lay the score line along a table edge and bend down gently, once per side. The labels on the bottom of the board read differently from the seller's picture: VIN+ / VIN− is the same as DC+ / DC−, and TRIG/PWM is the same as PWM+. Each J1 label covers a pair of holes; either hole in the pair works.

USB unplugged for all of this.

  1. Prepare the inverter lead. Cut the inverter's USB cable about 10 cm from the plug, keeping the long end on the inverter. Strip 5 mm: red is +5 V, black is ground. If there are also green and white wires, trim them short and cover the ends with hot glue.
  2. Inverter to the output block (top). Red under OUT+ (right screw), black under OUT− (left screw). Tighten until a tug won't pull them out.
  3. Make two power leads. Cut a red and a black male–male jumper in half and strip 5 mm off each cut end. Keep the halves with pins.
  4. Power to the input block (bottom). Red lead: bare end under the Feather's 5V screw (3rd from the top), pin end under the module's DC+ (VIN+, right). Black lead: bare end under the Feather's G screw (2nd), pin end under DC− (VIN−, left).
  5. Trigger. Push the male end of a male–female jumper into a J1 hole labeled PWM+ (TRIG/PWM on the bottom) and fix it with a dab of hot glue. Put the female end on the servo header's Sig pin (bottom pin).
  6. Ground link (recommended). Push a male pin into the J1 GND hole, glue it, and clamp its other end (stripped) under DC− next to the black lead. The board usually ties these together internally; this makes sure.
  7. EL wire into the inverter's output connector.
TestPlug in, wait for green, wave. The ring plays, the module's LED lights and the EL glows purple. About 5 seconds after the ring ends, the LED and the EL go dark together.

Run the bench checks

  • Ten separate waves, at least 12 seconds apart: exactly ten rings, each with the EL outline.
  • Wave continuously for a minute: a ring about every 10 seconds, stopping when you stop.
  • Point the sensor at a wall and leave the room for 30 minutes: no rings.
  • Pull the USB plug in the middle of a ring and plug it back in: amber for 60 seconds, then green, with nothing stuck on.

An extra ring about 10 seconds after each real one, with nobody moving, means the EL switching is disturbing the sensor's power. Raise COOLDOWN_S to 15 in code.py.

B

Carve the face

Everything that gets cut into the front. Page 4 of the print template is the full-size version of this drawing.

Respirator on, outdoorsBurning polyurethane foam gives off worse fumes than polystyrene. Work with the garage door open and a fan running, and stop every 30 minutes.

Print the template and trace the outline

  1. Measure the flat-ish front face of your pumpkin, between the moulding seams.
  2. Open print_template.html, enter your face size and a logo width about 50 mm narrower than the face (125 mm suits a 7 × 8 in face). Print at 100% ("Actual size", never "Fit to page") and measure the 100 mm bar with a ruler.
  3. Tape it over the face with the dotted line on the pumpkin's vertical center. Press a pencil or skewer along the grey outline every few millimeters so it leaves a dotted line in the foam.

Mark the holes

Still with the paper on, press a skewer through the center of each numbered circle and at the center of the motion-sensor circle. Peel the paper off, join the outline dots in pencil, and circle each hole mark.

Make every groove 3 mm wide and 3 mm deep

This covers both heads and the body outline.

  1. Bend the tip of the 5 cm bending knife into a U about 3 mm wide and 3 mm deep. Pulled slowly along a line, it melts a channel in one pass. If you'd rather, cut two lines 3 mm apart and lift out the strip between them.
  2. Test-fit the EL wire as you go. It should press in to just below the surface.
  3. Leave the foam bridges between each head and the body alone. They're what make the heads read as heads.

Cut the motion-sensor hole

A 24 mm circle all the way through, at the mark on page 4. From inside, check that the dome pokes through and the sensor's circuit board sits flat against the wall.

Make the three EL holes

Push a heated skewer or the wand straight through at each numbered center. Each hole carries the wire out and back in, so make them about 5 mm.

Look inside to check each one comes out where you expect.

Cut the speaker port and add feet

  1. Cut a hole about 35 mm across in the bottom, toward the front. The speaker's 20 cm jumper leads reach the board wherever it ends up.
  2. Glue three foam offcuts, about 12 mm tall, under the base so the sound can get out.

Open an access hatch

If the pumpkin's bottom already has an opening, use that. Otherwise cut a 10 × 10 cm hatch in the back, angling the knife inward so the plug rests on a lip. Cut a small notch in its bottom edge for the USB cable.

C

Light box and EL wire

One continuous run of wire, out and back through three holes.

Route the EL wire

The connector stays inside, so you'll be pulling the free end through every hole.

  1. Inside, leave about 25 cm of wire at the connector end to reach the inverter. Push the free end out through hole 1.
  2. Press the wire into the body groove all the way round and back in through hole 1. Tack it with hot glue every 3 cm or so.
  3. Inside, up the left side to hole 2. Out, around the big head, and back in through 2.
  4. Inside, over the top to hole 3. Out, around the small head, and back in through 3.

Let each glue dab set before you pull on the wire. Leave the inside runs loose rather than tight against the wall.

Cut and seal the end

Pull the last run snug inside, cut the wire about 2 cm past hole 3, and cover the cut end completely with hot glue. Throw the offcut away; a cut piece can't be powered again.

Test the outline

Plug the EL connector into the inverter on the bench, power the board, wait for green and wave.

TestThe whole outline lights. If a section stays dark, the wire is crushed or cracked there: look for a sharp bend or a glue dab that set while the wire was pulled tight.
D

Install the electronics

With only the sensor, speaker and EL to connect, put the parts wherever they're easy to reach through the hatch.

Put the parts in place

  1. Speaker face-down over the port in the base.
  2. The board, in its plastic box, hot-glued low on the inside front wall.
  3. MOSFET module and inverter on the floor beside it. Optional: encase the inverter in hot glue, which quiets its hum.
  4. Motion sensor into its hole from inside, dome out. Hot-glue the edges of its circuit board to the wall.

Reconnect everything

USB unplugged. Reconnect using the wiring table at the bottom of this page.

Run a final check

TestFive waves: five rings, each with the purple outline. Then unplug and plug back in once to confirm it starts up on its own.

Close it up

Hatch back in and taped, with the USB cable out through the notch.

E

On the porch

Set it up

Put it on the covered porch with the motion sensor facing the walkway, away from direct sun, vents and the grill. Keep the adapter dry. Plug it in; it's ready after the 60-second warm-up.

Test it at night

TestWalk up from the sidewalk five times. It should ring at 3 to 4 m, be clearly audible from about 5 m, and the logo should read from the street.

Tune it

IfChange
It rings too lateTurn the range dial (left) a little counter-clockwise for more range, then walk-test. If the range shrank instead, your board runs the other way.
Cars, passers-by or animals set it offTurn the range dial a little clockwise for less range, or aim the sensor lower.
Too loud or too quietVOLUME, from 0 to 1

Troubleshooting

Blinking red at power-on

A file is missing or a library is from the wrong bundle version. The serial window names the file. Re-run the install script after fixing it.

Serial says "EL_PIN and SENSOR_PIN are the same pin"

The sensor was moved to the servo Sig pin, which the EL now uses. Put PIR OUT back on the Btn terminal.

Never rings

Wait out the full 60-second warm-up. Check PIR OUT is on Btn, VCC on V+ and GND on G. Watch the serial window for TRIGGER to tell a sensor problem from a sound problem.

Rings by itself

Usually the sensor is still warming up, sees sun or a heat source, or its range dial is too high. On the bench, point it at a wall.

No sound, everything else works

Check the speaker leads are on − and +, not on the LED terminals, and that each twist is still tight on its tab.

EL stays dark or stays on

See the table under A8: the module's LED tells a trigger problem from a wiring problem.

Board restarts in the middle of a ring

A loose or thin USB cable, or a weak adapter. Use the 5 V 3 A adapter and the Anker cable.
